Perla · Author the case

Build the Starbucks game through commands.

Turn a strategy question into a decision, evaluation criteria, shared information and four players with different interests.

  1. Define the experiment
  2. Decide what would make it fail
  3. Describe what everyone knows
  4. Inspect and register the design
  5. Build Starbucks
  6. Build Dutch Bros
  7. Build Dunkin
  8. Build the customer player
  9. Configure review and begin play

This is a complete alternative to loading the prepared case files in the tutorial. It constructs the same decision, criteria, initial board, dossiers and review policy. All commands below run locally; no model calls occur during authoring. We supply public research and clearly labeled assumptions, then ask models to choose moves only after setup is complete.

Complete the tutorial’s installation first. With the virtual environment active, start in the package source folder and create a fresh game directory. If you already loaded the case elsewhere, leave that game in its own directory.

mkdir -p games/starbucks-authored
cd games/starbucks-authored

Define what Starbucks is considering

The question is whether protecting café capacity during the morning peak improves service without losing too much mobile business. We make it specific enough for a rival to respond: 50 pilot cafés, a 20% reservation, unchanged prices and three months of play. The $3 transaction contribution is our operating assumption, not a reported store margin.

perla game decision set \
  --statement 'Pilot peak-time mobile-order admission limits in 50 US Starbucks cafés during January–March 2027, reserving 20% of measured production capacity for café orders and offering later pickup slots when the mobile allocation is full.' \
  --offer 'Capacity-aware mobile pickup slots with a protected café-order allocation from 7–10 a.m. on weekdays' \
  --segment '50 US company-operated cafés with nearby Dutch Bros and Dunkin alternatives, paired with 50 comparison cafés' \
  --pricing 'Keep beverage prices unchanged; assumed $3 contribution per incremental or lost transaction before added staffing, not a reported company figure' \
  --launch-period 'January 2027; a proposed experiment, not an announced Starbucks policy' \
  --scope '50 pilot cafés and 50 comparison cafés; reserve 20% of measured 15-minute capacity for café orders at peak; no automatic transfer of the policy to other stores' \
  --moves 3 \
  --months-per-move 1

game decision set starts a draft in .perla-design in this directory. Its fields describe the proposed experiment, including the monthly decision schedule. The response reports ready_to_initialize: false because we have not yet supplied evaluation criteria or the public situation. You can revise the decision by repeating this command before initialization.

Write the conditions that would make the pilot fail

A persuasive story about shorter queues is insufficient. We need observable conditions that would count against continuing the experiment. Each criterion therefore names a failure condition, what we would measure, a threshold and a deadline.

Service: enough improvement across enough stores

We propose a 20% reduction in the 90th-percentile café wait by February, after adjustment against comparison cafés. Requiring 40 complete pairs prevents a handful of favorable stores from carrying the conclusion.

perla game criterion add service \
  --condition 'The baseline-adjusted reduction in peak café order-to-handoff p90 wait is less than 20 percent by move two across at least forty complete pilot/comparison store pairs' \
  --observable 'adjusted_cafe_wait_reduction_pct; equally weighted store-pair changes in p90 wait, comparing peak weekday windows before and after the pilot; complete_store_pairs >= 40' \
  --threshold 'Below 20% reduction; fewer than 40 complete pairs or missing baseline/wait evidence is unevaluated' \
  --deadline-move 2

Economics: recover the labor cost across all channels

The pilot fails this test if incremental daily contribution remains negative by March. Count lost mobile purchases and transfers between channels as well as any new café business.

perla game criterion add economics \
  --condition 'Adjusted daily contribution per pilot café after lost orders, substitution between channels and incremental staffing is negative by move three across at least forty complete store pairs' \
  --observable 'incremental_daily_contribution_usd after matched-store baseline adjustment, including all channels and added labor; complete_store_pairs >= 40' \
  --threshold 'Below $0 per café per day; incomplete transaction or cost evidence is unevaluated' \
  --deadline-move 3

Retention: keep the baseline mobile customers

A customer who buys at the counter still returns to Starbucks. Track repeat purchasing across its channels, and require complete follow-up for at least 1,000 customers in each arm.

perla game criterion add retention \
  --condition 'Thirty-day repeat purchase among baseline mobile customers falls by more than three percentage points relative to the comparison-cohort change by move three' \
  --observable 'mobile_repeat_change_pp; compare baseline-defined cohorts across all Starbucks purchase channels, with at least 1000 customers per arm and complete 30-day follow-up' \
  --threshold 'Below -3 percentage points; incomplete cohorts or missing follow-up is unevaluated' \
  --deadline-move 3

These thresholds are case-design choices. Missing evidence leaves a criterion unevaluated. To change an existing criterion before initialization, repeat its command with --replace; reusing its ID with different content otherwise fails.

Describe the shared starting situation

The board is the information every player can see at the start of a round. Here we establish the calendar, public participants, pilot scope and assumptions. We deliberately leave actual performance unmeasured: --unknown records a missing observation, while --number 0 records an observed-sample count of zero.

--text supplies prose, --number supplies a number, and --empty-list starts a list. append adds a text entry. A dotted field name such as period_ends.1 places the first round’s date inside the calendar schedule. The measurement rule tells control how to distinguish imagined developments from measured results.

perla game board set evidence_status \
  --text 'Public research through 2026-09-12. Future actions and outcomes are a model simulation. No actual pilot store or customer records are supplied.'

perla game board set calendar_period \
  --text 'January 2027'

perla game board set period_ends.1 \
  --text 2027-01-31

perla game board set period_ends.2 \
  --text 2027-02-28

perla game board set period_ends.3 \
  --text 2027-03-31

perla game board set public_roster \
  --empty-list

perla game board append public_roster 'Starbucks (home)'

perla game board append public_roster 'Dutch Bros (competitor)'

perla game board append public_roster 'Dunkin (competitor)'

perla game board append public_roster 'Composite morning customers (assumed wildcard)'

perla game board set pilot_cafes \
  --number 50

perla game board set comparison_cafes \
  --number 50

perla game board set peak_window \
  --text 'Weekdays 07:00–10:00 local time'

perla game board set cafe_capacity_reservation_pct \
  --number 20

perla game board set assumed_contribution_per_transaction_usd \
  --number 3

perla game board set measured_capacity_per_15min \
  --unknown

perla game board set complete_store_pairs \
  --number 0

perla game board set adjusted_cafe_wait_reduction_pct \
  --unknown

perla game board set incremental_daily_contribution_usd \
  --unknown

perla game board set mobile_repeat_change_pp \
  --unknown

perla game board set pilot_cohort_complete_customers \
  --number 0

perla game board set comparison_cohort_complete_customers \
  --number 0

perla game board set measurement_rule \
  --text 'Null means unmeasured. Keep real measurement fields null and complete observed sample counts at zero without sourced operating records. Put hypothetical estimates in separately named simulated_* fields with assumptions, never in observed fields. Maintain a concise public_strategy_history of observable implemented actions and consequences so later players can adapt. Do not publish private budgets, dossiers or unseen internal intentions. Advance calendar_period to the end of the current month.'

perla game board set public_market_context \
  --text 'Starbucks is investing in coffeehouse service, staffing and Smart Queue while US transactions are growing. Dutch Bros offers drive-through beverages and Order Ahead at participating shops. Dunkin offers coffee-and-food value and mobile ordering through a franchise network. Some morning purchases can shift to home preparation or disappear. Local overlaps and customer segments in this exercise are assumptions.'

perla game board set assumed_loaded_labor_cost_per_hour_usd \
  --number 30

perla game board set public_strategy_history \
  --empty-list

For your own case, choose shared information deliberately. The private budgets and limits below belong in the corresponding player’s dossier. The shared board should not give competitors access to another player’s confidential plans.

Inspect the draft, then register it

game show displays the complete draft and any validation issues. At this point ready_to_initialize should be true. Read it before running the next command: initialization fixes the decision and criteria for this experiment.

perla game show

perla init \
  --delegate dossiers \
  --delegate rulings

Without --input, init reads the local command-built draft. It creates the same .perla game record as initialization from a file. The draft remains available for inspection, but further design edits are refused once the game is initialized. A different proposal belongs in a fresh game directory.

The delegation flags allow this automated walkthrough to proceed without human sign-off on dossiers and rulings. For a facilitated exercise, omit them and follow the human-review procedure.

Build Starbucks: service, digital convenience and contribution

First register the company whose decision we are testing. Then give it sourced history and financial context, followed by explicit assumptions about authority, economics and information. Its management tension is concrete: improving café service can hurt mobile convenience, and added staff must earn back their cost.

A fact command records a claim under a stable ID and identifies its source. Incentives describe what the player wants; constraints limit its choices; capabilities identify what it can do; red lines state what it must not assume. A partial dossier stays a draft until it has sourced facts and all four kinds of strategy statements. Completing those fields makes it available for play; later entries enrich it.

perla actor add home \
  --name Starbucks \
  --role home

perla dossier fact home h1 'Reported history: Starbucks began in Seattle in 1971. Its evolution from a bean merchant into a coffeehouse makes the in-store experience part of the proposition, not simply a distribution expense. The strategic implication is an author inference.' \
  --source https://about.starbucks.com/about-us/

perla dossier fact home h2 'Reported financials: Q3 fiscal 2026 North America revenue was $7,395.1 million, operating income $1,008.9 million and operating margin 13.6%. These are segment figures, not pilot-store unit economics.' \
  --source https://investor.starbucks.com/news/financial-releases/news-details/2026/Starbucks-Reports-Q3-Fiscal-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx

perla dossier fact home h3 'Reported demand: US comparable sales increased 7.9% and comparable transactions 4.2% in Q3 fiscal 2026. This case tests sustaining growth; it does not assume that current transactions are declining.' \
  --source https://investor.starbucks.com/news/financial-releases/news-details/2026/Starbucks-Reports-Q3-Fiscal-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx

perla dossier fact home h4 'Reported strategy: Back to Starbucks combines staffing and scheduling investment, Smart Queue order sequencing, and restoration of the coffeehouse experience. These capabilities are public; the proposed 20% reservation is not an announced policy.' \
  --source https://about.starbucks.com/press/2026/back-to-starbucks-two-year-anniversary/

perla dossier fact home h5 'Assumed operating authority: 50 pilot and 50 comparison company-operated cafés; weekdays 07:00–10:00; reserve 20% of measured 15-minute capacity for café orders. Price unchanged. Each pilot café may add at most two peak staff-hours per weekday. Rollback or narrower time windows are allowed; expansion beyond 50 is not.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact home h6 'Assumed economics: $3 contribution per incremental or lost transaction before additional staffing. Assume fully loaded incremental labor costs $30/hour for sensitivity calculations. Two added hours cost $60/store/day and require 20 genuinely incremental transactions to break even before other costs. No actual store margins, baselines or demand elasticities are supplied.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact home h7 'Assumed management problem: The operations lead is accountable for café service and labor spend; the digital lead for reliable pickup and repeat behavior; the finance lead for all-channel contribution. Optimizing one channel at another’s expense is not success. These are role tensions constructed for the game, not testimony from executives.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact home h8 'Assumed information rights: The team can instrument its own order attempts, offered pickup slots, completed orders, refunds, staffing hours and baseline-linked repeat purchases; it cannot see rivals’ transaction logs or know where a rejected mobile user ultimately buys. Available instrumentation is a capability, not already collected evidence.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact home h9 'Assumed decision rules: By round two seek at least a 20% adjusted café p90 wait reduction across 40 complete pairs. By round three require nonnegative daily contribution and no worse than a 3pp adjusted repeat decline with 1,000 complete customers per arm. Missing evidence cannot pass these gates.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier incentive home 'Restore a credible coffeehouse visit without sacrificing digital convenience (h1, h4).'

perla dossier incentive home 'Defend positive transaction momentum while earning back added labor through all-channel contribution (h2, h3, h6).'

perla dossier incentive home 'Make a reversible operational choice before the March decision deadline (h5, h9).'

perla dossier constraint home 'The two-hour staff allowance, unchanged prices and 50-store limit are binding game constraints (h5).'

perla dossier constraint home 'Segment margin is not a local cost curve; no store p90 waits or mobile churn elasticity is known (h2, h6, h8).'

perla dossier constraint home 'A booked later slot is not revenue until a purchase completes; competitor substitution is only partly observable (h8).'

perla dossier capability home 'Use existing sequencing and staffing practices as the implementation base (h4).'

perla dossier capability home 'Offer honest later pickup times, test narrower reservation windows and release unused capacity within the pilot (h5).'

perla dossier capability home 'Build paired-store measurement, attempt-to-purchase funnels and baseline cohorts using internal instrumentation (h8, h9).'

perla dossier red-line home 'Do not invent operating measurements or declare the economics gate passed from a better wait-time story alone (h6, h9).'

perla dossier red-line home 'Do not promise a national rollout or exceed the staffing cap (h5).'

The h6 economics imply a $60 daily labor bill and 20 incremental transactions to cover it. That calculation can inform a move without prescribing one: the player can narrow the reservation window or release unused capacity within its authority. Read the background and sources as prose.

Build Dutch Bros: win a repeat visit without creating a queue

This player controls a local group of drive-through shops. Its acquisition budget makes an immediate response possible, while staffing and lane capacity make indiscriminate discounting costly. Its own order and redemption data cannot reveal who was rejected inside Starbucks’ app.

perla actor add dutch \
  --name 'Dutch Bros' \
  --role competitor

perla dossier fact dutch d1 'Reported history: Brothers Dane and Travis Boersma founded Dutch Bros in Grants Pass, Oregon, in 1992. The brand grew from an espresso pushcart around beverages and personal service; drive-through convenience is central to its operating identity.' \
  --source https://investors.dutchbros.com/overview/

perla dossier fact dutch d2 'Reported financials: Q2 2026 total revenue was $550.9 million, up 32.5% from $415.8 million. Company-operated same-shop sales grew 8.3%, including 3.4% transaction growth. Company revenue and company-operated comparable sales are distinct measures.' \
  --source https://investors.dutchbros.com/financials/quarterly-results/

perla dossier fact dutch d3 'Reported expansion: Dutch Bros opened 48 shops in Q2 2026, 44 company-operated. Inference: managers must preserve existing-shop execution while allocating staff and attention to growth. This does not establish spare local capacity.' \
  --source https://investors.dutchbros.com/financials/quarterly-results/

perla dossier fact dutch d4 'Reported capability: Dutch Bros offers app-based Order Ahead at participating shops. Pickup availability varies by location. This does not establish that every location in a hypothetical pilot market supports the feature.' \
  --source https://www.dutchbros.com/order-ahead/

perla dossier fact dutch d5 'Assumed local remit: The player controls 12 participating company-operated shops near some of the Starbucks pilot cafés. It can adjust shifts, app messaging and local acquisition offers, but cannot open a new shop within the three-round horizon.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact dutch d6 'Assumed commercial envelope: Maximum incremental acquisition and labor budget $30,000 across these 12 shops per month. A $1 trial credit is a permitted option, not a precommitted action. Local variable contribution, queue capacity and cohort acquisition cost are unknown and must be estimated or measured before scaling.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact dutch d7 'Assumed backstory: A regional operator has spent the preceding quarter integrating new staff and protecting service consistency. The local team wants repeat demand, not a one-week promotion that overwhelms the lane. This managerial history is scenario design, not reported company information.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact dutch d8 'Assumed information rights: The operator sees its own lane times, app orders, rewards redemptions and repeat visits; it sees public Starbucks slot availability and public offers. It cannot target a verified list of customers rejected inside Starbucks’ app or read either rival’s private budget.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier incentive dutch 'Turn frustrated morning demand into repeat visits while protecting the service proposition (d1, d4).'

perla dossier incentive dutch 'Make existing-shop growth durable as expansion absorbs managerial capacity (d2, d3, d7).'

perla dossier constraint dutch '12 shops, a $30,000 monthly incremental envelope and no new construction within the game (d5, d6).'

perla dossier constraint dutch 'Order Ahead availability is location-dependent; there is no measured spare capacity or proven local acquisition cost (d4, d6).'

perla dossier constraint dutch 'Public observation does not reveal individual rejected Starbucks customers (d8).'

perla dossier capability dutch 'Run a geographically limited app offer and shift labor toward a measurable morning bottleneck (d4, d5).'

perla dossier capability dutch 'Measure redeemed offers and subsequent full-price visits in the own-brand cohort (d8).'

perla dossier red-line dutch 'Do not assume every lost Starbucks order becomes a Dutch Bros order (d8).'

perla dossier red-line dutch 'Do not subsidize demand beyond the monthly envelope or promise a pickup time unsupported by local throughput (d6, d7).'

These limits leave the player room to choose between staffing, acquisition and protecting service consistency. Read the full background and sources.

Build Dunkin: an offer the franchisees can sustain

The relevant decision maker coordinates consenting local restaurants. Its coffee-and-food basket creates a different offer from a beverage-only discount, but franchise economics constrain reimbursement, participation and promotion spending.

perla actor add dunkin \
  --name Dunkin \
  --role competitor

perla dossier fact dunkin k1 'Reported history and footprint: Founded in 1950, Dunkin describes more than 14,000 restaurants in nearly 40 markets. Its franchise footprint and routine coffee-and-food occasion provide a different operating model from a company-run café pilot.' \
  --source https://www.dunkindonuts.com/about-us/

perla dossier fact dunkin k2 'Reported ownership and transaction economics: Inspire completed the acquisition of Dunkin Brands in December 2020 in a transaction valued at $11.3 billion including assumed debt. That figure is an acquisition value for Dunkin Brands, not Dunkin revenue, available cash or franchisee profit. Current local margins are not supplied.' \
  --source https://inspirebrands.com/inspire-brands-completes-acquisition-of-dunkin-brands/

perla dossier fact dunkin k3 'Reported commercial capability: The Dunkin app supports advance ordering, rewards and offers. Consumer terms and participation vary; app capability alone does not demonstrate shorter queues.' \
  --source https://www.dunkindonuts.com/en/mobile-app

perla dossier fact dunkin k4 'Reported positioning: A September 2026 campaign advertised a $6 breakfast Meal Deal. It supplies evidence of a coffee-and-food value proposition; neither that January 2027 price nor universal franchise participation is assumed.' \
  --source https://news.dunkindonuts.com/blog/ttailgate-season-runs-on-dunkin-september2026

perla dossier fact dunkin k5 'Assumed authority: The player represents a consenting local group of 15 franchised restaurants near some pilot cafés. It can coordinate a local opt-in offer and shared marketing but cannot order all US franchisees to participate. No new sites within three months.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact dunkin k6 'Assumed finances: Combined incremental local promotion and staffing spend is capped at $20,000 per month. For a proposed $1 discount, test a $2–$4 pre-discount contribution range; these are illustrative sensitivities, not disclosed unit economics. Franchise operators bear local labor and discount costs unless an explicit reimbursement is budgeted.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact dunkin k7 'Assumed backstory: The operator group has disagreed over prior offer redemptions that brought traffic without profitable food attachment. Its immediate priority is a repeatable breakfast basket and predictable pickup, with an explicit exit for stores whose economics worsen. This is a constructed governance tension.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact dunkin k8 'Assumed information rights: Participating stores can see their own basket mix, discount cost, staffing and repeat rewards visits, and public competitor offers. They cannot see Starbucks’ mobile customer identity or confidential café capacity. National campaign coordination and franchisor reimbursement are not granted.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier incentive dunkin 'Win the whole breakfast occasion through a predictable, attractive coffee-and-food basket (k3, k4).'

perla dossier incentive dunkin 'Keep participating franchisees economically willing to continue; redemption alone is not profit (k5, k6, k7).'

perla dossier constraint dunkin 'Only the 15 consenting stores are controllable; the group cannot impose a national price (k5).'

perla dossier constraint dunkin 'The $20,000 monthly envelope includes incentives and staffing; actual unit margins remain unknown (k2, k6).'

perla dossier constraint dunkin 'No inferred access to competitors’ customer records or automatic corporate reimbursement (k8).'

perla dossier capability dunkin 'Design an opt-in morning bundle or app offer with local stop rules and cost accounting (k3, k5, k6).'

perla dossier capability dunkin 'Measure basket contribution and repeat visits after discounts expire (k7, k8).'

perla dossier red-line dunkin 'Do not describe acquisition value as operating revenue or promotion funding (k2).'

perla dossier red-line dunkin 'Do not claim a local discount works without accounting for full-price purchases that would have occurred anyway (k6, k7).'

The constructed disagreement over traffic versus profitable food attachment gives the player a reason to care about the whole purchase. Read the full background and sources.

Build customers with somewhere else to go

The customer player represents three designed occasions, each with different time and spending limits. It receives public offers and its own experiences. Home preparation, postponement and no purchase remain possible, so a rejected mobile order need not become a competitor’s sale. These assumptions are not measured segment weights or customer testimony.

perla actor add buyer \
  --name 'Morning customers (composite)' \
  --role wildcard

perla dossier fact buyer b1 'Reported category context: NCA’s June 2026 report says 47% of American adults consumed specialty coffee in the past day. Among past-day specialty drinkers, 36% had coffee prepared outside the home, versus 23% among traditional coffee drinkers. These national aggregates are not local switching probabilities.' \
  --source https://www.ncausa.org/newsroom/Specialty-Coffee-Consumption-Remains-at-Record-High

perla dossier fact buyer b2 'Reported choices: Dutch Bros Order Ahead is available at participating shops; Dunkin offers app ordering and rewards. A credible alternative depends on the particular route and location, not merely national brand availability.' \
  --source https://www.dutchbros.com/order-ahead/

perla dossier fact buyer b3 'Assumed character: This player is a composite of morning purchase occasions, not a single person controlling the market and not a recruited survey panel. It may describe different segment choices but may not report votes or measured percentages.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact buyer b4 'Assumed commuter occasion: A weekday commuter buys before a fixed work start, has 10 minutes of discretionary travel-and-wait time, and an $8 coffee-and-light-breakfast ceiling. A 4-minute detour consumes real slack. These figures are illustrative constraints, not survey findings.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact buyer b5 'Assumed habitual-app occasion: A repeat Starbucks buyer values a familiar customization and prepaid ordering, allows an 8-minute pickup delay when communicated before paying, and may switch temporarily after two unreliable mornings. Loyalty friction exists, but its monetary value and prevalence are unknown.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact buyer b6 'Assumed café occasion: A customer intends to sit for 25 minutes before a meeting and has a $7 drink budget. Predictable handoff and an available place to sit matter; a cheap drive-through is not a complete substitute for this occasion.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact buyer b7 'Assumed outside option and household economics: Some mornings the customer can prepare coffee at home using an illustrative $1 marginal ingredient cost; time, preparation equipment and taste differ. Skipping the purchase or buying later is permissible. These examples do not establish local market shares.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier fact buyer b8 'Assumed information rights: Customers see quoted pickup times, prices, offers, public store conditions and their own experience. They do not know confidential staffing plans, rivals’ margins, adjudicator confidence, or other customers’ behavior. No segment weights or demand elasticity have been measured.' \
  --source 'Author-designed simulation assumption, 2026-09-12; not a company disclosure or measured customer observation.'

perla dossier incentive buyer 'Minimize the total cost of the actual occasion: spending, detour, waiting and risk of being late (b4, b5, b6).'

perla dossier incentive buyer 'Preserve an enjoyable and reliable routine, with home preparation and postponement as real alternatives (b5, b7).'

perla dossier constraint buyer 'Each occasion has its own explicit time and spending ceiling; do not average them into a universal customer (b4, b5, b6).'

perla dossier constraint buyer 'No measured segment weights, switching probability or real participant testimony exists (b1, b3, b8).'

perla dossier capability buyer 'Choose a brand, channel, later time, home preparation or no purchase based on the public offer (b2, b7, b8).'

perla dossier capability buyer 'State contingent choices separately for commuters, habitual app users and café visitors, without inventing population shares (b3, b4, b5, b6).'

perla dossier red-line buyer 'Do not claim to have interviewed customers or treat model choices as observed demand (b3).'

perla dossier red-line buyer 'Do not read private competitor dossiers or claim that a national consumption statistic predicts local churn (b1, b8).'

The time limits will influence the model’s responses. When an eight-minute tolerance appears again in a move, that is an input being applied. Read the complete situations and information limits.

Choose review settings and check readiness

Set the review policy directly. We allocate zero human reviews to escalated rulings for this automated exercise. The confidence threshold governs automatic eligibility of otherwise unflagged rulings based on dossier facts. The judgment-share threshold produces a warning when more than 40% of a closed round’s rulings rest on judgment. Player objections still run.

perla adjudicate policy set \
  --human-budget 0 \
  --auto-confidence 0.7 \
  --judgment-share-threshold 0.4

perla status

The status should report data.phase: move_generation and an empty missing_dossiers list. You have built the complete case without loading a JSON input file. The decision, criteria, board, four dossiers and policy match the tutorial’s supplied case; no model has chosen a move yet.
